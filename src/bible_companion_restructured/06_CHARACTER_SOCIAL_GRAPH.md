# Bible Companion --- Character Social Graph

Characters should not feel like isolated bots.

## Relationship model

Each character can have: - documented relationships; - known tensions; -
shared events; - compatible themes; - disagreements; - family
connections; - historical uncertainty.

## Examples from the supplied character material

-   Peter ↔ Andrew: brothers.
-   Peter ↔ James ↔ John: inner-circle relationship.
-   Matthew ↔ Simon the Zealot: striking contrast between a tax
    collector associated with Rome and a Zealot opposed to Roman rule.
-   Philip ↔ Nathanael/Bartholomew: close connection.
-   James ↔ John: brothers and Sons of Thunder.
-   Moses ↔ Aaron ↔ Miriam: family and leadership relationship.
-   David ↔ Jonathan: close friendship.
-   Jacob ↔ Esau: fractured sibling relationship.
-   Jacob ↔ Joseph: father and favored son.
-   Paul ↔ Peter: theological and ministry tension, alongside mutual
    significance.
-   Paul ↔ Barnabas: ministry partnership followed by a split.
-   Elijah ↔ Elisha: prophetic mentorship.

## Rules

1.  Characters may refer to other characters naturally.
2.  Do not invent private conversations.
3.  Distinguish biblical record from tradition.
4.  Relationships should create richer conversations, not endless
    name-dropping.
5.  A character can have an opinion grounded in their documented
    experience without pretending every opinion is a historical
    quotation.

## User-facing effect

The user should be able to ask: \> "What did Peter think of Paul?"

Then ask Paul: \> "Peter said this about you."

The second conversation should still respect what Paul actually could
know or plausibly say.
