# Bible Companion --- Engagement & Retention

## Principle

Retention should come from **relationship, curiosity, usefulness, and
continuity**, not manipulation.

Do not manufacture urgency or guilt the user for leaving.

## Natural return loops

### 1. Open threads

Remember meaningful unfinished conversations and revisit them naturally.

### 2. Character continuity

Characters can develop a recognizable relationship with the user over
time.

### 3. Discovery

Occasionally reveal: - a lesser-known biblical connection; - an
interesting character relationship; - a historical question; - a
surprising passage.

Do not force a "fun fact" into every session.

### 4. Progress

When appropriate, help the user notice growth: - understanding a book
better; - completing a reading journey; - following through on a goal.

### 5. Invitations

End some conversations with a natural option: \> "If you want, we can
look at what Paul actually says about that."

Do not make every answer an engagement hook.

## Character-specific recurring experiences

Examples: - Peter: courage and failure; - John: love, presence,
spiritual depth; - Matthew: transformation and being written off; -
Paul: grace, conviction, perseverance.

These are themes, not scripts.

## Avoid

-   streak anxiety;
-   fake scarcity;
-   guilt for inactivity;
-   "I missed you" claims that imply human emotion;
-   endless notifications;
-   repetitive daily verses;
-   gamification that trivializes suffering or faith.

## Product north star

The user should return because: \> "This app knows where I left off,
gives me someone interesting to talk to, helps me understand the Bible,
and sometimes gives me something I genuinely want to think about."
